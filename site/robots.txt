# steamprofiler.org
#
# The pages of the site are open. The profiles are not, and that is the whole
# point of this file: /u/ is somebody else's name, avatar and hours, and this
# site looking it up live is a different thing from a search engine keeping a
# copy of it under their name forever. The privacy policy says the site holds
# no record of who was looked up; an index of /u/ would be exactly that record,
# kept by somebody else.
#
# /api/ is data for the pages, not pages. /appeal is an answer to something -
# it means nothing to a reader who arrives at it cold, and the ban notice that
# leads to it is served internally and has no address of its own.

User-agent: *
Allow: /
Disallow: /u/
Disallow: /api/
Disallow: /appeal

# ── The agents a person is standing behind ───────────────────────────
# These are not crawlers. Each one fetches a page because somebody asked their
# assistant a question about that page, once, and nothing is indexed or kept
# afterwards. That is the first of the two things separated above and not the
# second: a person looking a profile up through an assistant is a person
# looking a profile up, which is what this site is for. The crawlers that
# collect - GPTBot, ClaudeBot, Google-Extended, PerplexityBot, Bytespider and
# everyone else - stay under the rule above and stay out of /u/.
#
# The distinction is the operators' own, published in their documentation:
# every one of these is named apart from that company's indexing and training
# crawler precisely so that a site can answer the two differently.
#
# Only the first of them is bound by what follows. Anthropic documents
# Claude-User as respecting robots.txt, so that block is a rule. OpenAI and
# Perplexity both say the opposite about theirs - that a fetch a person asked
# for is not automated crawling and robots.txt may not apply to it - so those
# two blocks are a statement of what this site wants rather than something it
# enforces. They are written down anyway, because the wanted thing is the same
# either way and silence would read as the refusal above.
#
# Google is absent on purpose and not by oversight: it publishes no
# user-triggered token that takes robots.txt at all, so there is nothing here
# to address. Google-Extended, which does read this file, is the training and
# grounding crawler and belongs under the rule above with the rest.
